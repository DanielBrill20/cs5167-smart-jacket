# User Needs and Interviews

## Interview Questions

1. What is your experience with smart technology?
2. What is your experience with wearable technology?
3. What is your process for choosing an outfit in the morning?
4. Tell me about your experience with jackets and when you wear them?
5. What are your pain-points with jackets during day-to-day use?

## Findings

- "Jacket" is an ambiguous term that means different things to different people. Some people think of hoodies, others think of lightweight outdoor coats, others think of heavy winter coats, and some think of indoor fashion like jean jackets.
- Some people only wear certain types of jackets, especially hoodies.
- Some people only own a single, multipurpose jacket.
- People like jackets for layering purposes. Having a jacket can provide warmth for cold outdoors or indoors and easily be removed once it's too warm.
- People want convenience. The people I interviewed did not spend much time picking out an outfit in the morning. In fact, the desire for convenience sometimes outweighs the desire to ensure garments match.
- People also pick outfits based on the weather when they wake up, or based on a weather app.
- One interviewee specifically mentioned not liking jackets because they're often ugly, bulky, and don't match with their clothes.
- Users are annoyed my garments with pockets or loops that stick out from the body and get caught on things like door handles.
- One interviewee mentioned their phone falling out of their jacket pocket because the way the pocket and opening is shaped.
- Many people are comfortable with wearable technology, but they often attribute it to a small selection of objects, particularly watches.
- People sometimes under-utilize smart technology. One interviewee discussed how they got into running and bought a smart watch to track their steps, sleep, etc. However, once they weren't running as much, they mostly just ended up using it like a regular watch to track the time. Over time, it lost its novelty and was forgotten about.
- Interviewees expressed "charging fatigue." Having to plug in multiple devices at night can mean that devices eventually just get forgotten and collect dust.

## User Needs and Design Requirements

- A smart jacket will likely have a target audience. A person who only wears hoodies won't want a jacket with buttons, for example.
- A jacket that can act as a chameleon and match with anything could be of value.
- Jackets need to "think of their surroundings." Designs should incorporate ways to ensure the jacket doesn't get caught on surrounding objects and pocketed goods are secured.
- The jacket should be easy to charge. Either being incredibly low power, charge by solar, or have some natural charging mechanism. For example, I'm thinking of how wireless earbuds naturally charge in their case. What if a hook or something charged the jacket magnetically?
